THE GRAVEYARD

A quantitative researcher is judged not by the strategies they trade, but by the strategies they have the discipline to throw away. Here is the autopsy of our failures.

"I'm always thinking about losing money as opposed to making money. Don't focus on making money, focus on protecting what you have." — Paul Tudor Jones

THE "ALMOST MADE IT" POST-MORTEMS

These strategies looked incredibly profitable at first glance. They hit fantastic metrics on one or two specific tests. But when subjected to the full 12 Rules, they failed. If you are a beginner, do not trade these. Here is exactly what killed them.

FATAL FLAW: COST-TO-RISK

NQ 1m 3-Red Candle Scalp

What looked good: 63.5% Win Rate on the Long side.

Why it failed: The median risk was 1.50 points. The round-trip cost was 1.0 point. The cost was 66% of the risk unit. Even winning 63.5% of the time, the Expected Value was mathematically negative because net wins were +0.5 pts and net losses were -2.5 pts. Wiped to $0.

REJECTED
FATAL FLAW: MAX DRAWDOWN

NAS100 Staggered Overnight

What looked good: Generated +21,300 gross points over 9 years.

Why it failed: Buying hourly from 10am to 11pm ET created a -32,026 point Max Drawdown. Overlapping 14 entries multiplies exposure to the exact same intraday swings. It takes 9 years of profit just to recover from the worst losing streak.

REJECTED
FATAL FLAW: OVER-LEVERAGE

Video Turtle Variant

What looked good: 31% CAGR claimed by the strategy video.

Why it failed: When we reverse-engineered the math, it was just 6x leverage on the standard daily Turtle. While it makes huge returns in a bull market, a 6x leverage blow-up immediately violates the 10% prop firm drawdown limit on the first bad trade.

REJECTED
FATAL FLAW: STOP WIDTH

ICT Low of Day (LOD) Reversal

What looked good: 28.4% Prop Pass Rate (Highest of all tested).

Why it failed: The 1-minute structural stop is 40 points wide. On a $5k account, one standard contract risks $800, instantly blowing the $250 daily loss limit (62% fail rate). It only works on Micro contracts (MNQ) or if the prop firm allows much wider daily losses.

REJECTED
FATAL FLAW: RUINOUS DRAWDOWN

120-Variation Pullback Grid (XAU 5m)

What looked good: +30% CAGR using a 3x ATR stop and 4R target.

Why it failed: It came with a -72% Max Drawdown. Grid optimizers will always find a way to make money by taking on insane risk. Without a Calmar ratio constraint, optimizing for maximum return is just a slow path to blowing up.

REJECTED
FATAL FLAW: TIME LIMIT

Confluence Sweep Short

What looked good: 55% single-account pass rate in early tests.

Why it failed: It was mathematically incompatible with the 30-day prop challenge. At 1% risk, it hits the 30-day time limit before hitting the profit target. At 2% risk, it hits the $250 daily loss limit due to intraday variance. It's an edge, but not a fast enough one for a sprint.

REJECTED
