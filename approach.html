F2 · METHODOLOGY & PROCESS

Approach

A systematic process designed to minimize unqualified risk and eliminate cognitive bias. Twelve rules, each one written plainly, with what it is, how it is applied, and what it protects against.

RESEARCH STANDARDS & THE 12 RULES
01
Split the data before you trust the result
In-sample / out-of-sample split at 2014 or 2021
WHAT IT IS

You divide the price history into two parts. The first part is where the strategy is allowed to learn and be adjusted. The second part is sealed off and used only once, at the end, as an honest exam.

HOW WE APPLY IT

The first 60% of the history is in-sample: we design, tune and iterate there. The final 40% is out-of-sample and we do not look at it until the strategy is frozen. It is opened once, and whatever it says is what we report.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It catches curve-fitting. A strategy that only works on the data it was tuned on has memorised the past rather than learned a behaviour. This split is the difference between a memory and an edge.

02
Assume you act one bar later than you would like
Signals shift by one bar, entry at the next bar's open
WHAT IT IS

A bar closes, and only then does the signal become knowable. In real trading you cannot buy at the close of the same candle that produced the signal, because that candle has already finished.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Every signal is computed on bar i, and the entry is taken at the open of bar i+1. No exceptions, even where it hurts the result.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It removes the single most common source of inflated backtests. Acting at the same close you measured is a fantasy that evaporates the moment real orders are placed, and it can turn a losing system into an apparently brilliant one.

03
Pay the costs you will actually pay
Realistic transaction costs applied to every trade
WHAT IT IS

Every trade crosses a spread and pays commission. On a two-point spread with a thirty-point stop, that is six percent of your risk gone before the market does anything at all.

HOW WE APPLY IT

A round-trip cost is deducted from every trade in the simulation: 0.05% on index products, 0.06% on gold. The figure is applied whether the trade wins or loses.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It kills strategies that only exist because trading was assumed to be free. Tight-stop, high-frequency systems look excellent until costs are applied and then often disappear entirely. Better to learn that in a spreadsheet than in an account.

04
Report the whole picture, not the flattering part
Full metric set: Sharpe, CAGR, max drawdown, win rate, profit factor, expectancy
WHAT IT IS

Any single number can be made to look good. Returns without risk, or win rate without position size, tell you almost nothing about whether a system is survivable.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Every backtest reports the same six metrics, every time, in the same order. A strategy is never described by its best metric alone.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It stops you being persuaded by a headline. A 30% annual return sounds excellent until you see it came with a 60% drawdown, which most people cannot sit through without abandoning the system at the worst moment.

05
Compare against doing nothing
Buy-and-hold reference over the same period
WHAT IT IS

If you had simply bought the index and left it alone, what would you have earned? That is the bar any active strategy has to clear, because it requires no skill and no attention.

HOW WE APPLY IT

The same instrument over the same dates is run as a passive benchmark and reported alongside the strategy, not buried beneath it.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It prevents celebrating a result that lost to the obvious alternative. A system returning 7% a year while the index returned 24% is not an edge, it is a worse way to own the same asset.

06
Prove the exit did the work, not luck
Random-direction baseline with identical exits and risk
WHAT IT IS

Take the same stop, the same target and the same position sizing, but choose long or short by coin flip. If that performs comparably, the entry logic is contributing nothing.

HOW WE APPLY IT

A thousand randomised runs are generated per strategy, and the real result is placed within that distribution. The question is whether it sits outside the noise.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It separates a genuine signal from the exit rules and risk model quietly carrying the whole result. Many systems that look clever are simply a decent stop-loss with a random entry.

07
Never blend the two halves together
In-sample and out-of-sample reported as separate columns
WHAT IT IS

A combined figure hides the most important thing in the entire test: whether performance held up once the strategy met data it had never seen.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Both periods are always shown side by side. If out-of-sample collapses, that is stated plainly rather than averaged away.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It exposes decay, which is the normal fate of most strategies. Averaging the two periods is how a system that worked in the past and stopped working last year still gets presented as healthy.

08
Test the account rules, not just the strategy
Prop-firm daily loss and total drawdown enforced in simulation
WHAT IT IS

Prop accounts fail you for breaching a daily loss limit or a total drawdown floor, regardless of whether the strategy is profitable over a year.

HOW WE APPLY IT

The specific firm's rules are encoded: 3% daily loss, 6% static floor, 6% target. The simulation stops the moment any of those is hit, exactly as the broker would.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It prevents a profitable strategy being taken to an account it cannot survive. A system can make money across a year and still be failed in week two by a single bad afternoon.

09
When the result looks too good, hunt the bug first
Suspicion of perfection before acceptance
WHAT IT IS

A backtest that looks extraordinary is far more likely to contain an error than to contain an edge. The correct instinct on seeing a beautiful equity curve is not excitement but audit.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Before any exceptional result is accepted, the code is re-read for overlapping positions, look-ahead, broken loop counters and impossible fills. The strategy is not discussed until the bug hunt is complete.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It saves days of work built on a number that was never real. Every inflated result eventually collapses, and the later it collapses the more has been built on top of it.

10
Never let the future leak into the past
No look-ahead under any circumstance
WHAT IT IS

A backtest must only use information that existed at the moment the decision was made. Reading tomorrow's price to decide today's trade produces a perfect system that cannot exist.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Every input is timestamped strictly before the decision point. Indicators are computed only from completed bars. Anything that would require knowing the future is removed rather than approximated.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It protects the validity of every other test. Look-ahead does not degrade a backtest, it invalidates it entirely, and it is usually invisible unless specifically hunted.

11
Write down what failed, and why
Every retired strategy logged with its reason
WHAT IT IS

A graveyard with reasons is institutional memory. Without it, the same attractive idea gets rebuilt and re-abandoned every few months.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Each retired strategy is recorded with its name, the instrument tested, the evidence, and the specific reason it failed. Retirements are never deleted.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It stops you paying twice for the same lesson. Over a long enough period the record of what does not work becomes more valuable than any single strategy that does.

12
Prefer boring parameters that survive a nudge
No curve-fitting; round numbers and tested neighbourhoods
WHAT IT IS

If a strategy only works at a target of 0.83R and a stop of 37 points, but fails at 0.8R or 40 points, it has been fitted to noise rather than discovered.

HOW WE APPLY IT

Parameters are chosen as round, defensible values, and then the surrounding combinations are tested. A real edge should still be visible when the settings are moved slightly.

WHAT IT SAVES YOU

It produces systems that keep working when the market shifts. Curve-fitted parameters break the moment conditions change, usually without warning and usually after capital has been committed.
